# Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio

DTI ratio measures how much of an applicant's income already goes toward
existing debt payments, expressed as a fraction (e.g. 0.30 means 30% of
income is already committed to debt). It is calculated as:

    DTI = existing debt payments / income

A lower DTI generally means an applicant has more room in their budget to
take on a new loan payment without financial strain. A higher DTI is a
signal of tighter finances, even if income itself is high, because more of
that income is already spoken for.

DTI is one of the engineered features fed into both the credit-score model
and the probability-of-default model. It is typically combined with the
requested loan's interest rate as a single interaction feature, since the
two together capture both how stretched an applicant already is and how
expensive the new debt would be.

There is no single universal cutoff, but many lenders treat DTI above
roughly 40-43% as a red flag for a new loan, since it leaves little buffer
for unexpected expenses or income disruption.
